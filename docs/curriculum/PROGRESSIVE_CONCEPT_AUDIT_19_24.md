- #22 should introduce 0-1 BFS as a special case of Dijkstra; #27 should contrast with time-dependent Dijkstra, not be taught as ordinary BFS.
- #16–19 are multi-source BFS and should be canonical in Topic 22. Keep short cross-links here rather than repeating full problem explanations.
- #23–24 form a good binary-search + BFS feasibility progression; prove the predicate is monotone and handle fire/person same-time boundary conditions.
- #32 and #33 are valuable implicit-graph optimization lessons: clear expanded buckets and model routes as nodes, respectively.
- #5 leaf peeling is a degree-layer algorithm, not a conventional shortest-path BFS; give it its own label.
- #37–39 are good method-selection diagnostics. Their value is learning when BFS is invalid or when a queue is serving a different algorithmic invariant.
- Final capstone should require designing a state with multiple dimensions and choosing BFS, 0-1 BFS, Dijkstra, or bidirectional BFS from edge costs and reversibility.

- #14–20 are useful targeted bug checks. Convert them to short assessment prompts attached to #1/#2/#9 rather than full problem rows.

## Topic 23 — Bipartite Graph / Matching

**Inventory:** 15 placements; 14 Medium/Hard and 1 Easy reference.  
**Verdict:** **Good progression from 2-coloring to DSU parity, matching, and Hopcroft–Karp. Some later problems are general graph/matching applications, not bipartite-coloring exercises.**

|---:|---|---|---|

| 2 | Possible Bipartition | Build conflict graph then apply 2-coloring | Core graph-construction transfer |
| 3 | Divide Graph into Two Equal Groups | Bipartite components plus global side-size allocation | Advanced integration; verify balancing argument |

| 6 | Trees Are Always Bipartite | Even/odd depth partition | Easy reference |
| 7 | Maximum Employees to Be Invited to a Meeting | Functional graph cycles plus incoming chains around 2-cycles | Cross-link Functional Graphs; not a matching problem |
| 8 | Detonate the Maximum Bombs | Directed reachability and maximum cascade | Diagnostic graph traversal |
| 9 | Maximum Matching of Players With Trainers | Greedy sorted matching for threshold-compatible pairs | Contrast: special matching has greedy solution |
| 10 | Campus Bikes | Globally sort all worker-bike pairs and greedily accept conflict-free pairs | Greedy assignment; not general bipartite matching |

### Recommended changes

- The clean progression is #1 → #2 → #5 → #11 → #12.
- Teach matching separately from coloring: bipartite coloring establishes two partitions; matching chooses disjoint edges; maximum matching requires augmenting paths.
- #9 and #10 have special greedy solutions; explicitly contrast them with general maximum bipartite matching so learners do not assume greedy always works.
- #3 needs verification: the current key insight's per-component balancing statement should be checked against the exact problem and proof.
- #4, #7, and #8 should be cross-topic diagnostics, not core bipartite problems.
- Add Konig's theorem / minimum vertex cover / maximum independent set relationship if the curriculum claims matching mastery. This is needed for applications such as Maximum Students Taking Exam.
- Hopcroft–Karp should include the phase invariant, shortest augmenting path layers, and complexity proof, not just code.
- Final capstone should compare greedy matching, augmenting-path matching, and Hopcroft–Karp under increasing graph size and adversarial examples.

- #11/#13 are implicit DAG DP; move canonical DP treatment to DP while preserving the graph interpretation.
- #14 is DFS/backtracking and should be a method-selection trap.
- #18–22 are short diagnostic checks attached to the relevant core problems, not independent learning objectives.
- Final capstone should combine DAG construction from a story, cycle detection, unique-order detection, and weighted longest-path DP.
